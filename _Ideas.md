This is your idea notebook. Write down story ideas here as they come, one `## Heading` per idea, separated by `---` rules. Length and polish don't matter; a two-line premise is enough.

When an idea feels ready, run `/new-story` and point it at the heading: it builds the full story scaffold (bible, timeline, folders) from what you wrote here.

The examples below are ideas that became published books. Delete them once you've added your own.

## [Memento Mori](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0G6Z2967X)


Josh made an app.

It tells you when you die.

It works.

An excavation, an ancient heirloom and a scooter accident trigger a series of events that unleash an app predicting people's deaths.
The mere option to know your death date transforms the world in unpredictable ways: people's behavior changes to the extremes, cults form, and society struggles to adapt to an unprecedented situation. Amidst the chaos, a woman tries to beat her ominous prediction and survive her death‑day.
Meanwhile, the origin of the app's source code is explored across the millennia in a journey where time's arrow is broken and the pieces are scattered all over the place.

## [The Book on the Porch](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H8KKB81B)

God and the Devil sit on a porch somewhere in Heaven, enjoying a perfect evening. God reads a book; the Prince of Darkness craves one of His divine cocktails. When God returns from the kitchen with the drinks, His book is missing, dropped by His old pal somewhere on Earth.

God freaks out. The consequences of humans reading the book might be catastrophic, the extent unknown even to the Almighty Himself.

Yet, like an Excalibur made of paper, the book defies them all: people come and go, and nobody proves worthy of fully reading the divine artifact. In an age of distraction, when people have forgotten how to read long texts, who will muster the focus and patience to read—really read—the Book of Enlightenment?

## [Crime at the Alphabet](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01KRYAJPE)

The Alphabet's peaceful routine shatters when someone kidnaps Lowercase Pi from his room. While all signs point to Lowercase Epsilon as the culprit, things are seldom as simple as they seem. Determined to find his son, Capital Pi ventures beyond the Alphabet's borders, unexpectedly joined by Final Sigma. Together they embark on a Kafkaesque adventure through uncharted realms, forcing them to question everything they thought they knew about their own existence.
